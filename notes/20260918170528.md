The Deno build of azk collapsed seven per-verb walks into one graph, then split them back. Sharing nodes across verbs in one graph was cheap; what it cost was the state. One walk means one State that must satisfy every node in every verb, which forced every per-verb field to be optional and made a union of per-verb result types uncompilable. Splitting restored required fields and dropped verb prefixes from node ids, since a position is only ambiguous when several verbs share a graph. The reuse that mattered survived: the unit of reuse is the node, not the graph position, so embed is written once and wired into four walks. The Rust port kept one walk per verb.
